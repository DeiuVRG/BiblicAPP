# Translation profile: Augustine, *De spiritu et littera* (412)

Profil de traducere: Augustin, *De spiritu et littera* („Despre spirit și literă”), 412. One
profile for both targets: the narrative is in English, the term table has a Romanian column and
the Romanian instructions are in Romanian (last section). English title: *On the Spirit and the
Letter*. Source language: Latin (`la`). Targets: English (`en`) and Romanian (`ro`), each made
from the Latin, never one from the other.

## The text and the edition

- Author: Augustine of Hippo (354–430); the work is addressed to **Marcellinus**, imperial tribune
  and notary, a friend of Augustine, who had written to him about a difficulty in the second book
  of *De peccatorum meritis et remissione* (that sinlessness is possible, though no one but Christ
  has attained it). The treatise defends that claim and then turns to the exegesis of 2 Cor. 3:6
  ("the letter kills, the Spirit gives life"): the Law as command without the gift of the Spirit
  is the letter; grace is the Spirit who pours out love (Rom. 5:5). Pelagius is **never named** in
  the treatise: do not add his name.
- **The Latin text** is the one printed by the Clarendon Press, Oxford, in 1914
  (*St. Augustine, De spiritu et littera, with an introduction by William Bright*), reprinted from
  W. Bright, *Select Anti-Pelagian Treatises of St. Augustine* (Oxford, 1880), with some variant
  readings from the Vienna *Corpus Scriptorum Ecclesiasticorum Latinorum* added. The spelling of
  the edition is `j` and `v` (*justitia*, *vivificat*), `charitas`, `ae` for the diphthong; the
  patterns of the glossaries cover both spellings.
- **Only the Latin of Augustine is the source.** Excluded from the source edition and never
  translated: Bright's introduction, the English chapter summaries and headings, the footnotes
  (variant readings, cross references), the marginal references to Scripture and to other works,
  running heads and page numbers. The chapter and section numbers of the edition are structure,
  not paragraph text.
- **The 19th-century English translations** (Peter Holmes and Robert Ernest Wallis in the
  *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers*, series 1, vol. 5, 1887, and the English volume of the
  inventory, P2D-12) are **references for the critic only**. They are never copied, never
  paraphrased, never the text from which a translation is made. The modern German translation
  of Otto Weber (P2L-10) is protected: not used.
- Tradition of the work: `undivided` (a Father of the West claimed by Catholics, Protestants
  and, in part, the Orthodox). Glossary rows for `all` and `undivided` apply.
- Sections: one section per chapter of the edition, headed `Caput I`, `Caput II`, ...; the
  translated heading is "Chapter I" (`headingEn`) and "Capitolul I" (`headingRo`).

## Register and voice

- A pastoral and polemical treatise of the year 412 by a trained rhetorician: long periodic
  sentences with subordinate clauses, antithesis (*littera* / *spiritus*, *lex* / *gratia*,
  *voluntas* / *adiutorium*), paronomasia (*gratia*, *gratis*, *gratuitus*), quotations of
  Scripture woven into the sentence, and frequent direct address to Marcellinus ("fili
  charissime Marcelline"). Keep the address (you, singular) and the first person singular of the
  author; the plural *nos* is the author with the Church.
- **Scripture is quoted by Augustine from the Old Latin and from memory.** Translate his Latin as
  his text (`quote-author-rendering`). Do not translate the Vulgate, and do not use an English
  or Romanian Bible text. Where the quotation is introduced by *ait Apostolus*, "the Apostle"
  (Paul) is "the Apostle" in English and "Apostolul" in Romanian.
- **Psalm numbers are those of the Vulgate** (for example Ps. 142 for Ps. 143): see the notes
  that the jobs carry. The Latin of this treatise has no inline citations in the text of the
  author; where one occurs, the job gives the converted form.
- *homo* is "human being" in general statements, "man" for the humanity of Christ ("not only man
  but also God by nature"); Romanian *om* in both.

## Traps observed in the text

- *lex* and *legere* are homographs in forms (*legis*, *lege*, *legem*): read the verb.
- *Spiritus* in 2 Cor. 3:6 is the Holy Spirit; *spiritus* in the discussion of "spiritual
  interpretation" (ch. 4) is the sense of a figurative passage: translate by sense.
- *ut* + subjunctive: purpose ("so that"), result ("so that" / "with the result that") or
  indirect command; do not use "that" alone.
- A chain of participles and ablative absolutes (*accepta occasione*, *lege veniente*) is
  rendered with clauses (see `calques-en.json`).
- The Latin of the treatise has no section numbers inside the paragraph; do not add "§".

## Exemplar passage (tone)

Latin: "Absurdum enim tibi videtur dici, aliquid fieri posse cujus desit exemplum."
English: "For it seems absurd to you to say that something can happen for which an example is
lacking."
Română: „Căci ți se pare absurd să spui că poate să se întâmple ceva pentru care lipsește un
exemplu.”

## Indicații pentru traducerea în română

- Reguli generale: `rules-ro.md`; glosar: `glossary-ro.csv` (rândurile pentru lucrări protestante
  nu se aplică, tradiția lucrării fiind `undivided`; rândurile acestei lucrări sunt cele `augustin-*`,
  legate de slug-ul ei, cu echivalentele din coloana Română de mai sus). Duhul Sfânt (nu „Spiritul Sfânt”), Hristos,
  Isus: ortografia numelui Domnului este o decizie deschisă pentru lucrările fără tradiție
  unică; se folosește „Isus”, ca în restul bibliotecii, până la hotărârea proprietarului.
- Textul biblic citat de Augustin se traduce ca text al autorului, din latina lui, nu după
  Cornilescu, Sinodală sau altă ediție; se marchează `quote-author-rendering`.
- Numerotarea psalmilor rămâne cea a Vulgatei (nu se convertește la numerotarea ebraică).
- Referințele biblice se scriu în notația românească din job (`Mat. 4:17`, `In. 3:5`), nu în cea
  latină.
